# Paper-Type Adaptation

First identify the paper's primary genre. Many papers combine genres; select a primary type and note secondary types. Do not force an empirical-method template onto every paper.

## Method or algorithm paper
Focus on:
- problem formulation;
- prior methods and gap;
- architecture and components;
- objective/function/algorithm;
- training and inference;
- traceable official source-code implementation when available;
- complexity and implementation details;
- baselines, ablations, robustness, and reproduction.

## Theoretical or mathematical paper
Focus on:
- definitions and assumptions;
- theorem statements;
- intuition behind the result;
- proof strategy and dependency between lemmas;
- critical proof steps;
- scope and conditions;
- examples/counterexamples and implications.

Do not invent missing proof steps. Label supplementary intuition separately from the authors' proof.

## Empirical or experimental paper
Focus on:
- research question and hypotheses;
- study design;
- participants/samples/data;
- variables and measurements;
- controls and confounders;
- statistical methods;
- results, uncertainty, robustness, and validity threats.

## Survey or review paper
Focus on:
- scope and selection criteria;
- taxonomy of the field;
- relationships among approaches;
- comparison dimensions;
- unresolved disagreements and open problems;
- limitations of the review process.

Do not describe a survey as if it introduced every method it discusses.

## Dataset, benchmark, or resource paper
Focus on:
- motivation and intended use;
- data collection and provenance;
- annotation and quality control;
- dataset composition and bias;
- splits and leakage risks;
- benchmark protocol and metrics;
- baseline results and limitations;
- access, licensing, and ethical constraints.

## Systems or engineering paper
Focus on:
- requirements and design constraints;
- architecture;
- components and interfaces;
- workload and deployment setting;
- performance metrics;
- scalability, reliability, cost, and failure modes;
- implementation and reproducibility;
- code-level mapping from architecture or pseudocode to inspected functions when available.

## Position, perspective, or conceptual paper
Focus on:
- thesis;
- definitions and conceptual framework;
- supporting arguments and examples;
- assumptions and counterarguments;
- implications and falsifiability;
- what is proposed versus empirically established.

## Qualitative or interpretive research
Focus on:
- research question;
- theoretical lens;
- sampling and context;
- data collection;
- coding/interpretation method;
- reflexivity and positionality where relevant;
- evidence excerpts;
- transferability and limitations.

## Case study
Focus on:
- case selection;
- context;
- timeline and interventions;
- evidence sources;
- causal claims versus descriptive findings;
- alternative explanations;
- transferability.

## Mixed-methods or interdisciplinary work
Explain how the different methods or disciplines fit together. Define terms from each field and identify where assumptions or standards differ.

## Genre-specific report adaptation

If a section does not apply, replace it with the relevant form of evidence:
- experiments → proofs, observations, case evidence, textual analysis, or synthesis;
- algorithm → conceptual framework, study protocol, proof strategy, or system design;
- ablation → sensitivity analysis, counterargument, robustness check, or comparative analysis.

Explicitly say when a conventional section is not applicable rather than filling it with generic text.
