# Value Assessment Framework

## Purpose

Decide what the paper is worth learning and where reading time should go. Separate:

1. **Academic value:** what the work contributes to its field.
2. **Beginner learning value:** what a novice can learn from it.
3. **Goal-specific relevance:** whether it helps a particular project. Assess this only when the user states the project or research goal.

Never collapse these into one unexplained score.

## A. Academic value

Score each dimension from 1 to 5. Give a short rationale, supporting evidence, and confidence (`high`, `medium`, `low`). Use `not assessable` when the paper alone cannot establish the answer.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem significance | Narrow or weakly motivated | Meaningful problem for a subfield | Important bottleneck or consequential question |
| Contribution/originality | Incremental or unclear | Useful extension or combination | Substantive conceptual, methodological, empirical, or theoretical contribution |
| Evidence quality | Weak or insufficient evidence | Reasonable evidence with gaps | Multiple appropriate forms of strong evidence |
| Generality/transfer | Highly narrow | Useful in several settings | Broadly reusable principle or method |
| Foundational potential | Little indication of downstream use | Could inform follow-up work | Establishes a framework, result, dataset, or method likely to support later work |

Do not assign a high score solely because of a famous author, venue, citation count, or confident abstract. If using citation counts or community reception, verify them from current external sources and report the date and limitations.

## B. Beginner learning value

Score each dimension from 1 to 5. This is a generic novice assessment, not a personalized assessment of an unknown user.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Foundational leverage | Does it teach concepts that unlock many later papers? |
| Explanatory value | Can its ideas be explained through a coherent sequence and examples? |
| Method-learning value | Does it demonstrate a reusable way to formulate or solve a problem? |
| Research-process value | Does it teach problem framing, experimental design, proof strategy, measurement, or argumentation? |
| Transfer potential | Can the learned ideas plausibly be applied beyond this exact paper? |

For each score, explain the reason. Avoid false precision: scores are structured judgments, not objective measurements.

## C. Goal-specific relevance

If no project or research goal is provided, write:

> Project-specific relevance: not assessed because no target project or research question was supplied.

Do not infer a personal research direction from the paper or prior conversation.

## D. Reading priority

Assign one priority and explain it:

- **P0 — Deep-study candidate:** unusually high foundational, methodological, or learning value. Worth studying the core mechanism and evidence carefully.
- **P1 — Important:** learn the central question, contribution, method/argument, and main evidence.
- **P2 — Supporting:** learn its role, key result, and relationship to other work; avoid unnecessary detail.
- **P3 — Low immediate priority:** retain the basic takeaway and reason for deprioritizing it.

Priority is not a claim about objective paper quality. A P3 paper may still be important to a specialized project.

## E. Value-to-time decision

Provide:
- What is worth learning from this paper?
- Which sections/components deserve the most time?
- Which parts can be skimmed?
- What would justify upgrading or downgrading its priority?
- A rough time range for orientation and for reaching the recommended mastery target.

Do not promise precise reading times. State assumptions and uncertainty.
